The Missed-Call Texter

$400 once

FOR: ANY ONE-MAN CREW WHOSE PHONE RINGS WHILE BOTH HANDS ARE BUSY

The pain. Every call he can’t take is a customer calling the next name on Google.
Sixty seconds. That’s all it takes to hand a $400 job to a competitor. He knows it
happens. He can’t quantify it, which makes it worse.
The fix. The moment a call goes unanswered, the customer gets a text within ten
seconds: “Hi, this is Dave’s Plumbing — we’re on a job right now. Can we call you
back in 20?” The customer feels seen. They wait. Dave books the job he would’ve
lost.
Line to remember: “It texts every missed caller back before they finish dialing the next
guy.”

The Review Robot

$300 once

FOR: ANYONE WITH GREAT GOOGLE REVIEWS AND ZERO REPLIES

The pain. Forty glowing reviews, not one response. To a customer scanning the list,
that reads as “this shop went quiet.” Future buyers notice. It quietly costs him jobs
every week and he has no idea.
The fix. Every new review gets a warm, personal reply within the hour — at 2 a.m.,
while he’s under a sink, on Thanksgiving. Five-star reviews get gratitude. Three-star
reviews get a professional answer that shows everyone else watching how he
handles problems. That second part is the part that sells the next customer.
Line to remember: “Your reviews stop looking abandoned. Reply #41 makes you look
open for business.”

03

The Invoice Chaser

$300–500 once

FOR: ANYONE WHO HATES THE MONEY CALL MORE THAN THE CRAWL SPACE

The pain. Nobody became a plumber to chase checks. So invoices sit. Thirty, sixty,
ninety days. The awkward call gets postponed forever, and the “good guy” reflex
quietly bleeds the business dry.
The fix. Polite, escalating reminders that run themselves. Day 3, a friendly nudge.
Day 10, firmer. Day 20, a final notice. The owner stays the good guy; the robot plays
bad cop. Most small businesses collect tens of thousands in old invoices within the
first month of running one of these.
Line to remember: “You never make the awkward call again. The robot makes it for
you, politely, forever.”

04

The 24/7 Receptionist

$297 / month

FOR: THE CREW THAT’S OUTGROWN VOICEMAIL

The pain. After-hours calls hit voicemail. Eight in ten callers just dial the next
company instead of leaving a message. He knows it. He’s tired. The phone rings
while he’s eating dinner with his kids.
The fix. A friendly voice answers every call, any hour. It answers basic questions
with his rules — the service-call fee, the service area, the emergency line. It books
them on his calendar. This is the only monthly product on the menu, and that’s the
pitch: one saved job a month covers her salary.
Line to remember: “She answers every call you miss, forever, for less than a phone
bill.”
